Adelyn Stroup

Director of Strategic Initiatives, CLCI

Adelyn has worked with CLCI for over 10 years, leading community engagement around Oyler and Lower Price Hill. She spearheaded the Resurgence Plan and is co-developing Ohio's first statewide community schools technical assistance center.

On the history

"In the late 1990s and early 2000s, CPS underwent a facilities master plan. The Ohio Supreme Court ruled that facilities alone were breaking the constitution for the right to a free and fair public education. Conditions were so poor students could not learn in them."

On the billion-dollar challenge

"One billion dollar price tag. Needed to pass a school levy to pay for the facilities master plan. No trust in the community in education — if you could send your kid somewhere else, you did. The community had to pay for 78%. They failed miserably."

On the CLC model

"We did not invent the community school model. But we have run with it. We call them Community Learning Centers because community schools are charters in Ohio. Belief that public schools are for the public."
"Business, senior citizens must too be involved. What is it you need in order to be successful?"

On the levy passing

"Under the new promise that every school is a CLC, and this line of questioning, the bond levy passed. Every levy has passed since by a pretty large margin. We are the only urban district in Ohio that is growing enrollment."

On Oyler as the flagship

"Oyler is our flagship and what this could look like. It used to be K-8. The community said they do not want the school to be torn down. We don't need a new fancy building, we need a high school."
"Could not find an Oyler student who had made it to a 10th grade seat in its 80-year history."

On financial sustainability

"What we didn't want is to do a build-out of a vision center, and then a superintendent says you can't have this anymore. Every partnership has to be financially self-sustaining. 14 agencies lead this at the site level."

On community engagement

"It has to be based on authentic community engagement. We do not say this is what you need. We build the infrastructure and the capacity for the community to say this is what we need."
"You have to have a resource coordinator. The lead agency and the partnerships are guarded at the site-level by a local decision-making committee."

On the whole child, whole family

"Most look at the whole child. We push it further — whole child, whole family. To create the conditions for learning, you are looking at the student level, and also the family and community level. 20% of a student's time is spent in the classroom. 80% is spent everywhere else."

On asset-based work

"What does a school district mean when they say community engagement? Usually, they mean you need to come to a parent-teacher conference. When we are reaching out to families, it can be deficit-based. But we are looking at how we turn it around to be asset-based."

On where CPS is now

"In the beginning, we couldn't even get students enrolled and we needed to pass this levy. We are there. Our levies are passing, people care about public education, and enrollment is growing."
"It's still bigger than 'what grade did you get in reading?'"
